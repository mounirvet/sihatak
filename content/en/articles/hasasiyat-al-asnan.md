That sudden jolt when you sip something cold or bite into ice cream is one of the most common dental complaints. The good news: most cases of tooth sensitivity are treatable once you know the cause.

## What is tooth sensitivity?

Sensitivity is a **sharp, brief pain** triggered by a stimulus, that fades quickly. It happens when the **dentin** — the layer beneath the enamel, containing tiny channels that lead to the tooth's nerve — becomes exposed. When dentin is exposed, triggers like cold or sweetness reach the nerve and cause pain.

## Common triggers

- Cold foods and drinks (ice cream, cold water).
- Hot drinks (coffee, tea).
- Sweet or acidic foods.
- Cold air when breathing through the mouth.
- Sometimes just touching the tooth while brushing.

## What exposes the dentin?

| Cause | How it happens |
|---|---|
| Enamel wear | Aggressive brushing, hard brush, frequent acidic foods |
| Gum recession | Exposes the root, which isn't protected by enamel |
| Tooth decay | Breaks through enamel to reach dentin |
| Cracked tooth | Exposes the inner layers |
| Teeth grinding | Wears down enamel over time |
| After a procedure | Temporary sensitivity after a filling or whitening |

## How is sensitivity treated?

### At home

- **Desensitizing toothpaste**, used consistently for a few weeks.
- A **soft brush** and gentle technique to avoid further enamel wear.
- **Cutting back on acidic foods**, and waiting 30 minutes before brushing after them.
- Avoiding excessive whitening, which can temporarily increase sensitivity.

### At the dentist

If it doesn't improve, a dentist can apply concentrated fluoride or desensitizing agents, and treat the root cause — a filling for decay, treatment for gum recession, or repair of a crack.

## When is sensitivity a warning sign?

Brief sensitivity is common, but see a dentist if it persists or worsens despite home care, if it becomes **constant pain** that doesn't fade, if it's focused on one tooth (which may signal decay or a crack), or if it comes with swelling or pain when biting.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist for sensitivity that lasts more than a couple of weeks, constant pain, or sensitivity in a single tooth. Identifying and treating the cause is the only way to resolve it for good.
