Most people brush their teeth every day — but many don't brush *effectively*. Good technique removes the plaque that causes decay and gum disease, while poor technique can leave plaque behind or even harm your teeth. Here's how to do it right.

## The basics: how often and how long

- Brush **twice a day** — morning and before bed.
- Brush for a **full two minutes** each time.
- Use a **soft-bristled** brush and **fluoride toothpaste**.

The nighttime brush is especially important, because saliva flow drops during sleep, leaving teeth more vulnerable.

## The correct technique, step by step

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| Angle | Hold the brush at 45° to the gumline |
| Motion | Gentle, short back-and-forth or small circular strokes |
| Coverage | Clean outer, inner, and chewing surfaces of every tooth |
| Gumline | Pay special attention where teeth meet gums |
| Tongue | Brush your tongue gently to freshen breath |
| Duration | Two minutes total — about 30 seconds per quarter of the mouth |

## After brushing: a key tip

Spit out the excess toothpaste, but **don't rinse your mouth with water immediately**. Rinsing washes away the concentrated fluoride that protects your enamel. Letting a thin layer remain lets the fluoride keep working.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Brushing too hard** — damages enamel and pushes gums back.
- **Brushing too quickly** — two minutes is longer than it feels.
- **Using a hard brush** — soft bristles are gentler and just as effective.
- **Forgetting the gumline** — where plaque and gum disease start.
- **Keeping an old brush** — replace it every three months, or sooner if bristles fray.
- **Brushing right after acidic foods** — wait about 30 minutes to avoid eroding softened enamel.

## Manual or electric brush?

Both work well when used correctly. Electric brushes can make good technique easier — especially for children, older adults, or anyone who tends to brush too hard — but a manual brush used properly is perfectly effective. The best brush is the one you'll use correctly, twice a day.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist if your gums bleed regularly when brushing, if you notice sensitivity or receding gums, or if you're unsure about your technique — they can show you exactly how to improve it. Combined with regular checkups, good brushing is the foundation of oral health.
