Losing a tooth — whether to decay, gum disease, or injury — is common, and modern dentistry offers several good ways to replace it. Replacing a missing tooth isn't only about your smile; it protects your remaining teeth, your bite, and your jawbone.

## Why replace a missing tooth?

A gap may seem harmless, but over time it can lead to:

- **Neighboring teeth drifting** into the space.
- The **opposing tooth over-erupting** (growing longer).
- **Bite and chewing problems**.
- **Jawbone loss** in the empty area, since the bone is no longer stimulated.
- Effects on **speech and appearance**.

Replacing the tooth helps prevent this cascade of problems.

## The main replacement options

| Option | What it is | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Dental implant | Titanium post in the jaw + crown | Replacing one or more teeth; healthy jawbone; most natural result |
| Bridge | False tooth anchored to neighboring teeth | Adjacent teeth are strong; faster than an implant |
| Partial denture | Removable replacement for several teeth | Multiple missing teeth; budget-friendly |
| Full denture | Removable replacement for all teeth | All teeth missing in a jaw |

## Dental implants

An implant is a small **titanium post** placed in the jawbone to act as an artificial root, topped with a crown. It's widely considered the closest thing to a natural tooth: it stands alone without affecting neighboring teeth, and it helps preserve jawbone. It requires adequate bone and a healing period, and is typically the higher-investment option.

## Bridges

A bridge "bridges" the gap by anchoring a false tooth to the teeth on either side. It's faster than an implant and doesn't require surgery, but it involves preparing (shaping) the neighboring teeth.

## Dentures

Dentures are removable replacements — **partial** for several missing teeth, or **full** for an entire arch. They're the most budget-friendly option and can replace many teeth at once, though some people find them less stable than fixed options.

## How to choose

The right option depends on how many teeth are missing, the health of your jawbone and neighboring teeth, your overall health, and your budget. There's no universally "best" choice — only the best one for your situation.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist soon after losing a tooth, even if it's not visible when you smile — earlier replacement helps prevent neighboring teeth from shifting and bone from being lost. A dentist can examine your mouth and explain which replacement options suit you best.
