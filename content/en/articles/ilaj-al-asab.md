Few dental procedures carry a scarier reputation than the root canal. But the reality is the opposite of the myth: a root canal **saves your tooth** and relieves pain — it doesn't cause it. Here's when you need one and what actually happens.

## What is a root canal?

Inside every tooth is a soft tissue called the **pulp**, containing nerves and blood vessels. When deep decay or a crack reaches the pulp, bacteria infect it — which is very painful and can lead to an abscess.

A root canal means the dentist removes this infected pulp, cleans and disinfects the canals inside the tooth's roots, and seals them. The tooth stays in place and keeps functioning, just without a living nerve inside.

## When do you need a root canal?

Signs that may point to it:

- **Severe pain** when chewing or applying pressure.
- **Prolonged sensitivity** to hot or cold that lingers after the stimulus is gone.
- **Discoloration** of the tooth (darkening).
- **Swelling or tenderness** in the nearby gum, or a pimple-like bump.
- Pain that throbs or wakes you at night.

Sometimes there are no clear symptoms, and the dentist finds the need through examination and X-rays — so diagnosis is made by a professional, not guesswork.

## What happens during a root canal?

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Anesthesia | Full local anesthesia so you feel no pain |
| 2. Access | A small opening in the tooth to reach the canals |
| 3. Cleaning | Removing infected pulp, cleaning and disinfecting the canals |
| 4. Filling | Sealing the canals with a special material |
| 5. Restoration | Filling the tooth, and usually placing a crown to protect it |

It may take one or more visits depending on the case.

## Is it really painful?

This is the biggest fear, and the reassuring answer is: **usually not.** With modern local anesthesia, the procedure feels much like having a large filling. The pain people associate with root canals is usually the **infection that came before** — and the treatment is what relieves it. Mild tenderness for a few days afterward is normal and easily managed.

## Why a crown matters afterward

A root-canal-treated tooth loses its blood supply and becomes **more brittle** and prone to fracture — especially back teeth that handle chewing force. That's why dentists often recommend protecting it with a **crown**, which can extend the tooth's life for many years.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist promptly for severe or persistent tooth pain, lingering sensitivity, or gum swelling. The earlier an infection is caught, the better the chance of saving the tooth with simpler treatment — and don't rely on painkillers to mask pain without addressing its cause.
