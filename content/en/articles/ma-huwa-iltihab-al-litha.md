Gum disease is one of the most common oral health problems worldwide, affecting most people at some point in their lives. The reassuring news is that in its early stage it is **fully treatable and reversible** if caught in time.

## What is gingivitis?

Gingivitis is irritation and inflammation of the **gums** — the pink tissue that surrounds and supports your teeth. The main cause is the buildup of **plaque**, a sticky film of bacteria that constantly forms on the teeth. When plaque isn't removed by regular cleaning, the bacteria release substances that irritate the gums and cause inflammation.

> **Gum disease in the Gulf — numbers that matter**
> Gum disease is more widespread in the region than many realize. A recent systematic review (2025) indicates that the prevalence of gum disease in Saudi Arabia is around **46%**, rising to **52%** among people with diabetes and **71%** among adults with obesity. These figures underline the importance of early prevention, especially for those with risk factors such as diabetes or smoking.
> *(Source: systematic reviews published on gum disease prevalence in Saudi Arabia, 2022–2025.)*

## What are the symptoms of gingivitis?

Watch for the following signs — any of them may indicate gingivitis:

- Redness instead of the healthy pink color.
- Swelling or puffiness of the gums.
- Bleeding when brushing or flossing.
- Persistent bad breath.
- Tenderness or sensitivity when touched.

Healthy gums don't bleed or hurt. Any recurring bleeding is a sign worth paying attention to.

## What causes gingivitis?

The primary cause is always **plaque buildup** from insufficient cleaning. But several factors raise the risk:

- Irregular or improper cleaning.
- Smoking, which weakens the gums' resistance to infection.
- Hormonal changes, such as during pregnancy or puberty.
- Some chronic conditions, such as diabetes.
- Certain medications that reduce saliva flow.

## How is gingivitis treated?

Before treatment, it helps to distinguish between the two main stages of gum disease, because the treatment and outcome differ dramatically:

| Comparison | Gingivitis | Periodontitis |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage** | Early | Advanced |
| **Tissue affected** | Gums only | Gums and supporting bone |
| **Bleeding** | Common when brushing | Common, may be spontaneous |
| **Bone loss** | None | Yes, progressive |
| **Reversibility** | Fully reversible | Bone damage is permanent (can only be halted) |
| **Treatment** | Better hygiene + professional cleaning | Deep cleaning and specialist care |
| **Risk of tooth loss** | No | Yes, if left untreated |

The key message from this table: **gingivitis is fully curable, but neglecting it turns it into an irreversible condition**. So early treatment makes all the difference.

Treatment depends on the stage of inflammation and usually includes:

### Improving home care

Brush twice a day with a soft brush, and floss once daily to remove plaque between the teeth. This alone is often enough to reverse early inflammation.

### Professional cleaning

If plaque has hardened into **tartar**, brushing won't remove it. A professional cleaning at the dentist is needed to remove tartar from the teeth and below the gumline.

### Follow-up

After treatment, sticking to daily care and regular checkups prevents the inflammation from returning.

## How can I prevent gingivitis?

Prevention is simpler than treatment and rests on three pillars: regular brushing and flossing, visiting the dentist every six months for a checkup and cleaning, and quitting smoking. These simple habits keep gums healthy for life.

## When to see a dentist

Book an appointment if bleeding or swelling persists for more than two weeks despite good care, or if you notice receding gums or loose teeth. Early intervention prevents the condition from progressing to a more serious stage.
