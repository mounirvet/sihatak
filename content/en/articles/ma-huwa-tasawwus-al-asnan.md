Tooth decay is the most common chronic disease in the world, affecting people of all ages. The good news is that it's largely preventable — and understanding how it forms is the first step to stopping it.

## What is tooth decay?

Tooth decay, also called **dental caries** or cavities, is the gradual destruction of tooth structure. It begins when **bacteria** in dental plaque feed on sugars and starches from food, producing **acids** as a byproduct. These acids attack the tooth's hard outer layer — the **enamel** — dissolving its minerals over time until a hole, or cavity, forms.

## How does tooth decay develop?

Decay doesn't happen overnight. It progresses through recognizable stages:

| Stage | What happens | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| Early demineralization | White spots appear as enamel loses minerals | Yes, with fluoride |
| Enamel decay | A cavity forms in the enamel | No — needs a filling |
| Dentin decay | Decay reaches the softer layer beneath enamel | No — needs a filling |
| Pulp involvement | Decay reaches the nerve; pain and infection | Needs root canal or extraction |

The key insight: **the earlier decay is caught, the simpler the treatment** — and the very first stage can even be reversed before a cavity forms.

## What causes tooth decay?

Several factors work together:

- **Frequent sugar and starch intake** — the more often, the more acid attacks.
- **Poor oral hygiene** that leaves plaque on the teeth.
- **Low fluoride exposure**, which weakens enamel's defenses.
- **Dry mouth**, since saliva normally neutralizes acids and washes away food.
- **Deep grooves** in back teeth that trap food and bacteria.

## What are the symptoms?

Early decay is often silent. As it progresses, watch for:

- White or brown spots on the tooth.
- Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet foods.
- Toothache or pain when biting.
- A visible hole or pit in the tooth.
- Bad breath or an unpleasant taste.

## How is tooth decay prevented?

Prevention is far easier than treatment and rests on a few habits: brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, flossing daily, limiting how *often* you consume sugary foods and drinks, and visiting the dentist regularly for checkups and cleanings. Fluoride — from toothpaste, water, or professional application — plays a central role in strengthening enamel against acid attacks.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist if you notice sensitivity, spots, or a hole in a tooth — and don't wait for pain, since pain means decay has already reached deeper layers. Regular checkups every six months catch decay in its early, easily treated stages.
