It sounds like a simple question, but how often — and *when* — you brush has a real impact on your dental health. Here's what the evidence supports.

## The simple answer: twice a day

For the vast majority of people, the recommendation is clear: brush **twice a day**, for **two minutes** each time, with **fluoride toothpaste**. Once in the morning and once before bed covers what your teeth need.

## Why the nighttime brush matters most

If you only remember one thing, make it this: **never skip brushing before bed.**

During the day, saliva constantly washes away food particles and neutralizes the acids that attack enamel. But during sleep, **saliva flow drops sharply**. That means any plaque and food left on your teeth overnight has hours to do damage, undisturbed. Brushing before bed clears that away.

## Is more than twice a day better?

Generally, no. Brushing twice with good technique is enough. Brushing too often — or too hard — can actually backfire:

- It can **wear down enamel** over time.
- It can cause **gums to recede**.

Thoroughness and gentle technique beat frequency and force.

## Timing around meals

One important nuance: **don't brush immediately after acidic foods or drinks** (citrus, soda, juice). Acids temporarily soften enamel, and brushing right away can wear it down. Instead, rinse with water and wait about **30 minutes** before brushing.

## Don't forget what brushing misses

Brushing twice a day cleans the surfaces of your teeth — but not between them. That's why **daily flossing** (or another interdental cleaning method) is an essential partner to brushing, reaching the spots a brush can't.

## When to see a dentist

See a dentist if your gums bleed regularly, if you have persistent bad breath despite good brushing, or if you're unsure whether your routine is effective. Alongside brushing twice daily, regular checkups keep your mouth healthy.
